TraceCharterBot

TraceCharterBot is the web crawler of TraceCharter. If you saw it in your logs, this page explains what it does and how to control it.

What it does

TraceCharter follows public cybersecurity news and research. For each new article, it records when the article first appeared, so that we can show how a security story spread and which sources reported it first.

The crawler reads about 38,000 cybersecurity websites. On each site it reads the home page, the RSS or Atom feed, the sitemap and robots.txt. It may also read article pages that these link to.

How it identifies itself

Every request carries this User-Agent:

TraceCharterBot/0.1 (+https://tracecharter.com/bot)

Requests come only from these addresses:

  • 57.129.78.17
  • 2001:41d0:701:1100::a284

Both addresses have the reverse DNS name miner.tracecharter.com, and that name resolves back to the same addresses. A request that claims to be TraceCharterBot from any other address is not from us.

How often it visits

  • Most pages: about once an hour.
  • Sites that publish many times a day: their feed may be checked every 5 to 30 minutes.
  • Never more than one request at a time to your site, and at least 10 seconds between requests.
  • It asks "has this changed?" first (conditional requests), so an unchanged feed costs you a few hundred bytes.
  • If your server answers 429 or 503, it slows down and follows your Retry-After header. It also follows a Crawl-delay line in robots.txt.

How to stop it, or limit it

TraceCharterBot follows robots.txt. To block it from your whole site, add:

User-agent: TraceCharterBot
Disallow: /

To block only part of your site, list those paths. If your robots.txt has no group for TraceCharterBot, it follows your User-agent: * group. It reads robots.txt again at least once a day, so a change takes effect within 24 hours.

You can also write to us, and we will stop visiting your site within two working days.

What it does not do

  • It does not log in or fill in forms.
  • It does not solve CAPTCHAs or try to get around bot protection. If your site blocks it, with robots.txt, an "access denied" answer or a bot check, it stays out.
  • Some pages show their content only with JavaScript. For those, it may load the page in a standard browser. It never uses a browser to get past a bot check.
  • It does not store the names or e-mail addresses of authors.
  • It does not use your pages to train AI models.
